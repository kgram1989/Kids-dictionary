# Master word list for the Kids Dictionary: the words to add, most-used first.
#
# How it was made: words that children typically know by about age 11, ranked by
# how often they are used in everyday spoken English. Inflected forms (dogs, ran),
# names and swear words were removed; days, months, numbers and a few other
# basics were added by hand.
#
# Sources (ratings are not reproduced here, only the resulting order of words):
#   - Kuperman, Stadthagen-Gonzalez & Brysbaert (2012), Age-of-acquisition ratings
#     for 30,000 English words, with SUBTLEX-US word frequencies.
#   - Brysbaert & Biemiller (2017), Test-based age-of-acquisition norms for
#     44 thousand English word meanings.
#   Both obtained from https://osf.io/kz2px/ under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. This file is
#   derived from that data and is shared under the same licence (non-commercial,
#   share-alike, with credit to the authors).
#
# Later, 562 words were added that the first method missed: common adverbs and other
# words the age research does not cover (already, finally, usually), and words that
# are common in children's books but rare in everyday speech (toil, beckon, tomb).
# They were chosen by hand from words used often in books for ages 7-12 in CYP-LEX
# (Korochkina, Marelli, Brysbaert & Rastle, 2024, https://osf.io/squ49, CC BY 4.0),
# leaving out British-only words, names and unsuitable words, and were placed where
# their frequency in those books ranks them. The earlier words keep their order.
#
# The list was then read through by hand and about 175 words unsuitable for young
# children were removed (swearing, slurs, sexual words, nudity, alcohol, tobacco, drugs,
# gambling, violent crime). Do not add them back without the owner's say-so.
#
# Second list: after the first 10,900 words were written, 4297 more were added at the
# end, again most-used first. They are words that appear in books for ages 7-12 in
# CYP-LEX (ranked by how often they are used in the books for ages 7-9 and 10-12) and
# that the age-of-learning data above shows children know by about age 12; -ly adverbs,
# which that data does not cover, were taken when their root word is in the dictionary.
# Every candidate was read by hand. Left out: names, British-only words and spellings,
# old-fashioned and rare words, and words on adult topics (the same topics as in
# data/blocked.txt, plus illness, weapons, crime, politics, religion and name-calling).
# The first list had missed some everyday words (could, should, most, fox, shore)
# because the research data files them as names or leaves them out; they lead this
# part. About 10,700 candidates were considered; the rest did not pass, so this part
# is shorter than the first.
#
# Third part: 1047 more words at the very end, from a wider search. About 15,500
# further candidates were read by hand, drawn from: 767 public-domain children's
# books on Project Gutenberg (its children's fiction, literature, fairy-tale, picture
# book, history, instructional and anthology shelves; only word counts were used),
# children's television subtitles (CBeebies and CBBC, from SUBTLEX-UK) and the
# Children's Printed Word Database, both as counted in CYP-LEX, the Simple English
# Wiktionary headword list and Simple English Wikipedia (CC BY-SA; used only to see
# which words appear, no text copied), and the rest of the CYP-LEX and age-of-learning
# lists. Most candidates were old-fashioned, adult or technical and were left out;
# what was kept is mainly school-subject words (science, maths, geography, grammar,
# music), animals, plants and foods, modern everyday words (podcast, emoji,
# smartphone) and adverbs common in children's books.
#
# Fourth part: 3516 school-subject words at the very end: language arts and grammar,
# maths, science, earth and space, social studies, civics, geography, music, art,
# sport and computing terms, and the names of animals, birds, fish, insects,
# dinosaurs, plants, trees and foods. They were gathered subject by subject, then
# checked against the subject categories in the English Wiktionary (CC BY-SA; only
# the category a word is filed under was used, no definitions) and kept only if the
# word also appears in the children's sources named above. About 11,000 candidates
# were read by hand. This part includes a few capitalised names that are taught as
# school topics: the planets, the continents and oceans, the geological periods,
# and Diwali, Kwanzaa, Ramadan, Passover, Eid and Juneteenth.
#
# The list has more than 10,000 entries on purpose: some will be skipped as not
# suitable for children (see data/skipped.txt). Do not reorder it.
# Run "npm run next" to see which words come next.
